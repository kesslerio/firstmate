Scenario: a generation proved by the remote's serve certificate must not be mistaken for one
the seat ledger failed to issue. Two halves: the positive (cert-derived generation with a
released, never-issued candidate in the ledger still counts) and the adversarial guard (a
record that names a generation the ledger never issued is refused).

Lab: primary $LAB/primary with remote ios served (certificate holder task=ios generation=gen-ios).
The ledger was seeded with a real failed first spawn through the product:
  $ FM_HOME=$LAB/primary bin/fm-fleet-seats.sh reserve ios --generation gen-old --kind secondmate --harness claude --model pool-model-a --holder-pid $$
  $ FM_HOME=$LAB/primary bin/fm-fleet-seats.sh release ios --generation gen-old --reason prelaunch
so the only ledger incarnation is a released candidate gen-old, and the running supervisor's
generation gen-ios exists only in the serve certificate.

PASS half (the intent clause): the primary's route record carries no generation; the product
derives gen-ios from the certificate and counts it once:
  $ FM_HOME=$LAB/primary bin/fm-fleet-seats.sh reserve task-y --generation g-y1 ...
  fleet-seats: reserved pool=shared id=task-y generation=g-y1 used=3 capacity=3
  [exit 0]

Adversarial guard: a record that names a generation its ledger never issued still fails closed
rather than silently counting:
  $ (append remote_spawn_gen=gen-bogus to the primary route record)
  $ FM_HOME=$LAB/primary bin/fm-fleet-seats.sh reserve task-z --generation g-z1 ...
  fleet-seats: no seat - task record $LAB/primary/state/ios.meta names generation gen-bogus, which its seat holder never issued (a pooled model is never launched without a counted seat; choose another route or repair the authority)
  [exit 5]

FAIL half (see evidence-finding-persisted-cert-generation.txt): once the product's own remote
generation resolver persists the certificate-derived generation into the route record, the same
exemption no longer applies and the fleet refuses admission.
